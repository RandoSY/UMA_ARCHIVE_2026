# Kitech / Kitchen Lab — One-Pager

**Pillar:** Measured World

## One-sentence purpose

Kitech turns the ordinary kitchen into a serious, low-cost science and computation learning environment where mass, temperature, volume, time, energy, and change become measurable.

## Who it serves

- Children and beginning students who need science to start with visible things.
- Adults who want to re-enter science through ordinary life rather than intimidation.
- Teachers who need accessible, low-cost measurement labs.
- Families who can use home objects as authentic learning instruments.

## Where it happens

The kitchen counter, stove, sink, dining table, pantry, notebook shelf, laptop corner, and ordinary home workspace.

Home is not a lesser lab. It is the nearest lab.

## What it measures

| Quantity | Typical instrument | Learning doorway |
|---|---|---|
| Mass | kitchen scale / smart eScale / HX711 | conservation, calibration, density, food, energy |
| Volume | measuring cup / DIY graduated cylinder | density, fluid measurement, error |
| Temperature | thermometer / DS18B20 / thermistor | cooling, heating, thermal equilibrium |
| Time | clock / timer / microcontroller | rates, change, experimental procedure |
| Energy | kettle, microwave, toaster, calorimeter | conservation, power, heat transfer |

## Flagship activities

- Tea Time and tea extraction.
- Newton's Cooling.
- Predictive Mixing.
- Cost of boiling water.
- Pop-can calorimeter.
- Smart eScale calibration and uncertainty.
- DIY graduated cylinder and density work.
- Water clock and mass-to-time linkage.

## Computation role

Computation enters after the physical meaning is visible:

```text
estimate → measure → record → graph → model → explain
```

Spreadsheets, browser dashboards, CSV files, Python/Jupyter notebooks, and simple models all become extensions of the kitchen measurement loop.

## Why it matters

Kitech proves that science begins where life already happens. The student does not need expensive black-box equipment to begin thinking scientifically. The student needs a question, an estimate, an instrument, and a way to explain what happened.
