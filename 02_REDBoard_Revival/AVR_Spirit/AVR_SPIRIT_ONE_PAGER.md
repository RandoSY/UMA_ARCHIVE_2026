# AVR Spirit — One-Pager

**Pillar:** Measured Machine / REDBoard Revival

## One-sentence purpose

AVR Spirit names the educational ethos of low-cost, 8-bit, close-to-the-metal learning through Arduino Nano / ATmega328P / REDBoard-style systems.

## Who it serves

- Students learning real microcontroller behavior without hidden complexity.
- Teachers who want a large ecosystem of examples, shields, and recoverable mistakes.
- Makers who value simple serial output, visible wiring, and practical repair.
- Nugget / REDBoard Revival work that needs a sturdy classroom bridge.

## Where it happens

Arduino Nano bench, Uno shield-world, REDBoard Revival PCB, breadboard, KiCad project, serial terminal, or browser dashboard.

## What it emphasizes

| Theme | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Visible wiring | Students can trace what connects to what. |
| Serial truth | Text output reveals behavior directly. |
| Shield-world | Practical connectors, pin maps, and reusable boards. |
| Low cost | Failure and recovery are part of learning. |
| Documentation | Large ecosystem and abundant examples. |

## Relationship to Nugget

AVR Spirit complements PIC Black Box. PIC gives a compact inspectable edge processor; AVR/Nano gives a classroom-friendly bridge with USB serial, shields, libraries, and familiar Arduino workflows.

## Why it matters

AVR Spirit is not nostalgia. It is a deliberate learning stance: stay close enough to the machine that students can understand cause and effect, but use enough ecosystem support that they can build real things.
